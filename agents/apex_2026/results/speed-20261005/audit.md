# Speed and timing audit (read only)

Scope: official source and existing **development** receipts/traces only. Distances and speeds below use simulator world units, abbreviated m and m/s. No new episode, holdout inspection, or source change. The frozen guarded preview remains a research artifact.

## Time and camera facts

- `agents/apex_2026/evaluate.py:145-209` measures `lap_time_ms = round(1000 * (raw.finish_time_s - post_warmup_start_time))`. Physics runs at 50 Hz and the wrapper repeats each action for four raw frames, so one decision lasts 0.08 simulated seconds (`core/vendor/car_racing.py:55-65,539-559`; `env_wrapper.py:41-65`). Wall time, import time, and action latency do **not** enter lap time. Import has a 10 s limit; reset and each action have 5 s limits; an error becomes an operational failure, not a driving DNF (`evaluate.py:145-184,275-305`). A finish also needs the official qualified forward finish-line crossing, not progress alone (`core/finish_line.py:35-99`).
- After the 50-frame warmup, camera zoom is fixed at 16.2 surface pixels/world unit. The 1000×800 surface is scaled to 96×96, then to 84×84 grayscale (`core/vendor/car_racing.py:610-649,798-802`; `env_wrapper.py:7-10`). Car center is about `(42,63)` pixels, with 1.3608 lateral and 1.701 forward pixels/world unit. The image top is about 37 m ahead; the metric parser's highest sampled road row is about 34 m ahead (`guarded_preview_agent.py:3030-3035,3090-3128`). At 100 m/s, that parser horizon is about 0.34 s, or 4.25 decisions.
- Official white HUD height is `0.02 × true hull speed` in the original render, where speed is the norm of Box2D `hull.linearVelocity` (`core/vendor/car_racing.py:717-750`). In the final 84×84 image the bar is about 2.1 px wide and 0.042 px high per m/s. The metric decoder reads `frame[74:83,10:13]` and uses `(mass-0.20)/0.0882`, clipped to 180 m/s (`guarded_preview_agent.py:3080-3087`). Against the prior R7 mandatory traces, phase-aligned HUD versus stored true speed has mean absolute error 0.61–0.71 m/s across four tracks, maximum 2.22 m/s. It is reasonably calibrated over the observed 0–90 m/s range; its crop/clip is unvalidated above roughly 180 m/s.

## Physical and controller limits

- The car code has no explicit speed cap, **but Box2D does**: installed `box2d-py 2.3.5` exposes `b2_maxTranslation = 2.0` world units per raw step. At the official 0.02 s step this is an effective 100 m/s translation ceiling, also seen as a stable maximum in the independent physics diagnostic. Gas is clipped to `[0,1]`; rear-wheel gas rises at most 0.1 per physics frame, reaching a full command from zero in about 0.20 s. Engine power is 40,000 in the source's units and torque falls as wheel angular speed grows (`core/vendor/car_dynamics.py:17-22,136-142,175-235`; `core/vendor/car_racing.py:55-65,539-559`). Front steering joints are mechanically limited to ±0.4 rad and the steering motor speed to 3 rad/s before damage scaling (`car_dynamics.py:117-129,148-158`). Larger action steering magnitudes cannot increase the final wheel angle.
- Each road wheel's combined longitudinal/lateral force is capped at 400; grass gives 240, before damage effects. The four hull polygons and four wheel fixtures imply about 7.30 mass units, so 1600/7.30 ≈ 219 m/s² is only a **loose upper bound** on combined road acceleration, not a sustainable cornering rate. Engine torque, rear-wheel drive, force sharing, slip, steering response, obstacles, and damage reduce what is usable (`car_dynamics.py:17-108,160-235`; `damage.py:3-36`). The candidate's `lateral_accel=145` and `braking_accel=100` are controller assumptions, not official constants.
- The public metric controller caps its target cruise at 130 m/s and its default is 100. It limits wheel-angle request by `atan(3.24×145/v²)`, uses `sqrt(145/curvature)` as a corner target, and caps gas at 0.25 or 0.16 when modeled lateral demand exceeds 50% or 75% of 145 (`guarded_preview_agent.py:3038-3058,3163-3222,3270-3289`). For example, at 90 m/s with a 0.05 rad request, modeled demand is about 125 m/s², so gas is capped at 0.16 even though the action contract permits 1.0. This is a potential pace limit, not proof that more throttle is safe. The robust component defaults to 68 m/s on clear straights and 30–48 m/s curve targets, with additional hazard slowing (`guarded_preview_agent.py:914-1035,2967-3017,3303-3314`). Prior R7 mandatory traces report a target below 60 m/s on 82–86% of decisions.
- The nominal 100 m/s² braking budget deserves calibration against emitted brake actions. In the existing contact-free mandatory R7 traces, decisions with brake ≥0.3 and prior speed >40 m/s show median speed reduction about 110 m/s², while brake ≥0.1 shows median about 59 m/s². These are observed total-speed changes, not isolated longitudinal traction measurements. They show why assuming 100 for every mild brake command can underbudget approach distance.
- The road has half-width 6.667 m; six physical obstacle circles have radius 1.2 m. The hull extends 2.6 m forward and 1.2 m sideways, and wheel contacts also count (`core/vendor/car_racing.py:63-66,702-715`; `core/track_variables.py:11-16`; `car_dynamics.py:25-108`). Conditional example: at 100 m/s, one decision consumes 8 m. Slowing to 60 m/s at an **assumed effective** 100 m/s² needs another 32 m; adding 2.6 m of front reach needs 42.6 m, beyond the metric parser's ~34 m road horizon. Earlier visual cues or a safer path are needed for such a transition. This does not establish an unavoidable speed ceiling.

## What 13 seconds would require

Existing fresh-hybrid mandatory development traces imply 280, 336, 309, and 279 road tiles, respectively. At the official 3.5 m centerline step (`car_racing.py:63,329-348`), estimated centerline lengths are below. Racing lines can change the driven distance, so these are planning estimates.

| Track | Centerline m | Mean for 13 s, m/s | Mean for 10 s, m/s | Fresh-hybrid time s | Observed peak m/s |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | 980.0 | 75.4 | 98.0 | 19.62 | 92.1 |
| 2 | 1176.0 | 90.5 | 117.6 | 26.20 | 96.6 |
| 3 | 1081.5 | 83.2 | 108.2 | 22.90 | 89.7 |
| 4 | 976.5 | 75.1 | 97.7 | 21.82 | 94.6 |

The required **average** speed, especially on track 2, is near the Box2D 100 m/s ceiling and near or above prior observed **peak** speeds, before accounting for launch, bends, and six obstacles. An independent privileged-state physics probe reports 0→99 m/s in 2.42 s, with 1.108 s of distance deficit relative to starting at 100 m/s. Combining that deficit with its reported route lengths (986.8/1181.8/1088.7/967.8 m) gives illustrative 10.98/12.93/12.00/10.79 s. These are **not** proven lower bounds or legal-camera laps: racing lines, curvature, obstacles, and launch control change the distance and achievable speed. The 13 s goal is consequently very tight, particularly on track 2, but feasibility is not established either way. A legal-camera high-speed study should separately measure true speed versus HUD, emitted gas/brake/steer and curvature, contact/off-road outcomes, and whether the speed gain survives all four mandatory geometries. The unchanged sparse global curvature fallback can miss a localized bend (`guarded_preview_agent.py:3215-3222`), so speed increases need explicit sparse-road checks.

No source-use/quota tool is exposed in this environment.
